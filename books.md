Here is a list of selected books that I found interesting. 

## *Darkness Visible: A Memoir of Madness* by William Styron

I like how the author describes his subjective experience with depression in detail throughout the book even though he believes the experience is beyond description.  

He describes it as self hatred, failure of self-esteem, disorder of mood, mysteriously painful and elusive in the way it becomes known to the self, does not disappear overnight, a great mystery, gloom crowding in on me at mid afternoon, a sense of dread and alienation, stifling anxiety, feeling my mind is dissolving, confusion, failure of mental focus, lapse of memory, obliterating any enjoyable response to the living world, trance of supreme discomfort, focused on the immediate upheaval, takes over your voice and makes it ancient, judgment if life is worth living, different for every person, the shadows of the nightfall seems more somber and mornings less buoyant, numbness, pervasive hypochondria, the fading evening light had none of its familiar autumnal loveliness, but ensnared me in a suffocating gloom, immense and aching solitude, feeling the wind of the wing of madness, disruptions in sleep with a complete absence of dreams, wondering and writhing at the devastation taking place in my mind, being imprisoned in a fiercely overheated room, being alone causing exquisite panic and trepidation, life slipping away, all sense of hope vanished, no idea of a futurity, both the solitary actor and lone member of the audience, anguish, 

Loss in all of its manifestation is the touchstone of depression, loss of self esteem and self reliance,  

The faith in death as the eventual respite, in deliverance, in ultimate restoration, is dead. 

He had become suicidal--he later explains--because of the medication he was taking. 
Brahms Alto Rhapsody pierced his heart like a dagger and made him feel again. 

Hospital was his salvation. A sanctuary where peace can return to mind. The real healers were seclusion and time in the hospital. He did art therapy in there. 

He emphasizes that the experience of this disease is idiosyncratic and individual and there is no universal cure. 
Mysterious in its coming, mysterious in its going, the affliction runs its course and one finds peace. 

Friends, lovers, family, admirers can help prevent suicides. It requires a religious devotion to persuade the sufferer of life's worth. 

He speculates lots of other causes: his 60th birthday as a milestone of mortality, genetics, death of his mother when he was thirteen, incomplete mourning. 

It *is* conquerable. 

He finishes with Dante in hell and his ascent: 
In the middle of the journey of our life 
I found myself in a dark wood, 
For Iihad lost the right path 
... 
And so we came forth, and once again beheld the stars.
